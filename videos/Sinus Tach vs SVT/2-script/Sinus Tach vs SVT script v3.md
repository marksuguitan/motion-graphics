# Sinus Tach vs SVT: Answering or Looping? (script v3)

**Audience (assumed):** EMS providers and medical/nursing learners.
**Format:** 16:9, 1920x1080, narrated by markusguapo (ElevenLabs eleven_v4, default settings, no tags). About 2:45.
**Sources:** StatPearls, "Supraventricular Tachycardia" (updated Feb 2025) and "Sinus Tachycardia." Atrial rate in flutter from StatPearls "Atrial Flutter" (NBK540985). The EMS1 article is no longer used.

**Our structure:** one idea runs through the whole video. Sinus tach is the heart *answering* a need. SVT is the heart *stuck in a loop*. We build the conduction pathway first, run each rhythm on it, then let the mechanism predict the ECG and the treatment.

Visual notes in brackets are a rough plan for the storyboard, not final.

## 1. Cold open: one strip, one question (0:00)
[A single lead II strip prints and scrolls. Narrow, regular complexes. Rate readout: 170. No title yet.]

Narrow complexes. Perfectly regular. A rate of one seventy.
Is this heart answering a call? Or is it stuck in a loop?

[Title lands: "Sinus Tach vs SVT."]

## 2. The normal route (0:12)
[Mark's heart-chambers.svg. The pathway lights up in order: SA node, atria, AV node (a visible hold), His-Purkinje, ventricles.]

Every normal beat follows the same route. It starts in the SA node, high in the right atrium, and spreads across both atria. Then it reaches the AV node, which holds the signal for about a tenth of a second, so the atria can finish emptying. From there it races down the His-Purkinje system into the ventricles.
That fast lane is why the QRS is narrow. A narrow-complex tachycardia tells you the ventricles are still using it, so the problem starts at or above the AV node. Both rhythms in this video start up here.

## 3. Sinus tach: the heart answering (0:45)
[The same pathway, faster. A row of causes slides in beside the heart: exercise, pain, anxiety, then hypoxia, blood loss, dehydration, sepsis.]

In sinus tachycardia, nothing about the route changes. The SA node is just firing faster than one hundred, because the body is asking for more output.
Sometimes the reason is harmless, like exercise, stress, pain, or anxiety. Sometimes it's serious, like low oxygen, blood loss, dehydration, or sepsis. A fast resting heart rate can be one of the earliest signs that something is wrong.
On the E C G, it looks like a normal beat, only faster. An upright P wave in leads one, two, and A V L, in front of every QRS.

## 4. SVT: the heart in a loop (1:20)
[Zoom to the AV node. Two loop diagrams, side by side.
AVNRT: a small circuit inside the node.
AVRT: down the AV node, back up an extra pathway at the edge of the ventricle. A pulse travels each loop, and the ventricles fire on every lap.]

Most S V T works differently. It's re-entry: a signal caught in a circuit, going around and around.
In the most common type, A V N R T, the circuit sits inside the AV node itself. In A V R T, the heart has an extra pathway between the atria and the ventricles. The signal goes down through the AV node, and back up the extra pathway.
Every lap fires the ventricles. And because the loop isn't following the body's needs, it starts suddenly, runs fast and regular, usually between one fifty and two twenty, and stops just as abruptly.

## 5. What the mechanism predicts (2:00)
[Two strips stacked, same rate. Sinus: P wave marked before each QRS. SVT: no P wave to find. Then a three-row comparison builds: Trigger, Start and stop, P waves.]

So put them side by side.
Sinus tach has a reason you can find, and it follows that reason. S V T often starts out of nowhere, sometimes after caffeine, stimulants, or stress, and it switches on and off.
And in A V N R T, you often can't find a P wave at all. It's hidden inside the QRS.

## 6. The AV node is the key (2:20)
[The AVNRT loop again. A vagal maneuver, then adenosine, briefly blocks the AV node, and the circulating pulse stops. Sinus rhythm resumes on the strip.]

That loop is also how we stop it. Vagal maneuvers and uh-den-oh-seen both slow conduction through the AV node. If the circuit runs through the node, blocking it for a moment breaks the loop.
For a stable patient, start with a vagal maneuver, like val-sal-vuh, lying flat. Then uh-den-oh-seen, by rapid I V push. An unstable patient gets synchronized cardioversion.
Sinus tach has no loop to break. Slowing the rate doesn't fix the reason it's fast. Treat the cause.

## 7. Close (2:45)
[Back to the opening strip at 170. The two words resolve: "Answering" and "Looping."]

So when you see a fast, narrow, regular rhythm, ask one question. Is the heart answering, or is it looping?

## Accuracy check (all StatPearls)
- **AV node delay of about 100 to 120 ms, so the atria can empty:** SVT, Pathophysiology.
- **Narrow QRS means activation through His-Purkinje, so the origin is above it:** SVT ("at or above the AV node," QRS <120 ms).
- **SA node in the high right atrium:** standard anatomy, not stated in either article.
- **Sinus tach is over 100 with catecholamine triggers (exercise, stress, pain, anxiety), plus serious causes (hypoxia, hemorrhage, dehydration, sepsis):** Sinus Tachycardia, Etiology.
- **"Tachycardia at rest may be among the earliest signs of serious illness":** Sinus Tachycardia, Pearls.
- **Sinus P waves upright in I, II and aVL, each followed by a QRS:** Sinus Tachycardia, Evaluation.
- **Most SVT is re-entry through the AV node or an accessory pathway:** SVT, Pearls.
- **AVNRT is the most common type, with the circuit within the AV node; orthodromic AVRT goes down the AV node and back up the accessory pathway:** SVT.
- **Triggers include caffeine, stimulants, alcohol and stress; onset is sudden and termination abrupt; usually 150 to 220 and regular:** SVT.
- **AVNRT often has no discernible P waves, hidden in the QRS:** SVT, Evaluation.
- **Vagal maneuvers slow AV conduction, done supine; Valsalva:** SVT, Treatment.
- **Adenosine blocks the AV node and interrupts the circuit, given by rapid IV push, first-line:** SVT.
- **Synchronized cardioversion if unstable:** SVT.
- **Treat the cause of sinus tach:** Sinus Tachycardia ("the cornerstones of management").
- **Left out on purpose:** drug doses, WPW and pre-excited AF, flutter and AF look-alikes, ablation. Any of these could become a short add-on or a follow-up video.
