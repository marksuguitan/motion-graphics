# The Oxyhemoglobin Dissociation Curve
Audience: medical students
Review this narration script for medical accuracy. For each problem, quote the line, say what is wrong, and cite a source.

## Scene 1: Hook
**1A** · Every red blood cell has two jobs. [S1]
**1B** · Pick up oxygen in the lungs. Then let it go in the tissues. [S1]
**1C** · One graph shows how hemoglobin does both. The oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve. [S1]

On-screen text: Lungs · Tissues · SO2 readout · The oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve · Hemoglobin carries 98% of blood oxygen [S1]

## Scene 2: Axes
**2A** · Along the bottom is the partial pressure of oxygen, PO2, in millimeters of mercury. It reflects the oxygen dissolved in the blood. [S2]
**2B** · Up the side is hemoglobin saturation, the percentage of hemoglobin's oxygen binding sites that are filled. [S2]

On-screen text: PO2 (mmHg) · PO2 = dissolved O2 · Hemoglobin saturation (%) · SO2 = Hb binding sites filled

## Scene 3: Cooperativity
**3A** · Hemoglobin has four subunits. Each one holds a heme group that can bind one oxygen molecule. [S1, S2]
**3B** · The binding is cooperative. Once the first oxygen binds, hemoglobin changes shape, and the next ones bind more easily. [S1, S2]
**3C** · That is why the curve is not a straight line. [S2]
**3D** · It is an S shape. A sigmoid curve. [S1, S2]

On-screen text: Adult hemoglobin (HbA) · α · β · heme · T state · R state · Remaining sites: higher O2 affinity · Sigmoid

## Scene 4: Landmarks
**4A** · In arterial blood leaving the lungs, PO2 is about 100, and hemoglobin is about 97 percent saturated. [S2; PO2 value: source pending]
**4B** · After blood passes through resting tissues, mixed venous PO2 is about 40, and saturation is about 75 percent. [S2; PO2 value: source pending]
**4C** · So on each pass through the body at rest, only about a quarter of the oxygen is unloaded. [S2]

On-screen text: Arterial: 100 mmHg, 97% · Mixed venous: 40 mmHg, 75% · At rest, per pass: (97 − 75) / 97 ≈ 23%, about 1/4 unloaded

## Scene 5: P50
**5A** · Halfway up the curve is a landmark called the P50. It is the PO2 at which hemoglobin is 50 percent saturated. [S2, S3]
**5B** · For normal adult blood, under standard conditions, it is about 27 millimeters of mercury. [S2, S3]

On-screen text: P50 · Normal adult blood · P50 ≈ 27 mmHg · Standard conditions: 37 °C, pH 7.4, PCO2 40 mmHg [source pending]

## Scene 6: Shape
**6A** · The top of the curve is relatively flat. Even if PO2 in the lungs falls from 100 to 60, saturation only drops to about 90 percent. [S2]
**6B** · That plateau protects oxygen loading. Below 60, though, the curve gets steep. [S2, S3]
**6C** · In the tissues, a small drop in PO2 releases a large amount of oxygen. That steep slope makes unloading efficient. [S3]

On-screen text: Plateau: protects loading · Steep: efficient unloading · PO2 and SO2 readouts

## Scene 7: Shifts
**7A** · The curve can also shift. The P50 tells you which way hemoglobin's grip on oxygen has changed. [S2, S3]

On-screen text: P50

## Scene 8: Right shift
**8A** · A shift to the right means lower affinity. Hemoglobin lets go of oxygen more easily, and the P50 rises. [S1, S2]
**8B** · Four common factors shift it right. A lower pH. More carbon dioxide. More 2,3-DPG. And a higher temperature. [S1, S2, S3]
**8C** · The effect of acid and carbon dioxide is called the Bohr effect. [S1, S2]
**8D** · Think of exercising muscle. It is warm, acidic, and full of carbon dioxide. That is exactly where oxygen needs to be released. [S1, S2]

On-screen text: ↓ pH · ↑ CO2 · ↑ 2,3-DPG · ↑ Temperature · Bohr effect · P50 36 (illustrative)

## Scene 9: Left shift
**9A** · A shift to the left is the opposite. Higher affinity. Hemoglobin holds on to oxygen more tightly, and the P50 falls. [S1, S3]
**9B** · A higher pH. Less carbon dioxide. Less 2,3-DPG. And a lower temperature. [S1, S2]
**9C** · Fetal hemoglobin also sits to the left. It binds 2,3-DPG poorly, which helps the fetus pull oxygen from the mother's blood. [S1]
**9D** · Carbon monoxide is different. It occupies oxygen binding sites, and makes the remaining sites hold oxygen more tightly. So the blood carries less oxygen, and releases less of what it carries. [S1, S2]

On-screen text: ↑ pH · ↓ CO2 · ↓ 2,3-DPG · ↓ Temperature · P50 20 (illustrative) · Fetal Hb (2α + 2γ) · Carbon monoxide · Blocks O2 binding sites → O2 capacity ↓ · Remaining sites hold O2 tighter → unloading ↓ · Curves shown are for normal Hb; with CO the maximum O2 content also falls.

## Scene 10: Summary
**10A** · To sum up. Right shift: lower affinity, higher P50, more oxygen released at a given PO2. [S2, S3]
**10B** · Left shift: higher affinity, lower P50, oxygen held more tightly. [S2, S3]
**10C** · An easy way to remember it: right means release.

On-screen text: More O2 released at the same PO2 · O2 held more tightly · Right means release · Footnote: Total O2 delivery also depends on blood flow, Hb concentration and lung loading. [S2]
