| # | Narration | On screen |
|---|-----------|-----------|
| 1 Hook | Every red blood cell has two jobs. Pick up oxygen in the lungs. Then let it go in the tissues. One graph shows how hemoglobin does both. The oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve. | Unchanged (logo dash into the red cell, capillary, title). |
| 2 Axes | Along the bottom is the partial pressure of oxygen, PO2, in millimeters of mercury. **It reflects the oxygen dissolved in the blood.** Up the side is hemoglobin saturation, the percentage of **hemoglobin's** oxygen binding sites that are filled. | Axes as v1. **Small notes under the axis titles: "PO2 = dissolved O2" and "SO2 = Hb binding sites filled".** |
| 3 Cooperativity | Hemoglobin has four subunits. Each one holds a heme group that can bind one oxygen molecule. The binding is cooperative. Once the first oxygen binds, hemoglobin changes shape, and the next ones bind more easily. That is why the curve is not a straight line. It is an S shape. A sigmoid curve. | Ribbon tetramer. **Header "Adult hemoglobin (HbA)".** **Binding is paced evenly (no speed-up).** **After the first O2 binds, the three empty hemes get a highlight ring and a chip: "Remaining sites: higher O2 affinity".** T → R stays a simplified schematic. |
| 4 Landmarks | **In arterial blood leaving the lungs,** PO2 is about 100, and hemoglobin is about 97 percent saturated. **After blood passes through resting tissues, mixed venous PO2 is about 40,** and saturation is about 75 percent. So **on each pass through the body** at rest, only about a quarter of the oxygen is unloaded. | Points "Arterial" (100, 97%) and "Mixed venous" (40, 75%) as v1. **Card: "At rest, per pass: (97 − 75) / 97 ≈ 23%, about 1/4 unloaded".** |
| 5 P50 | Halfway up the curve is a landmark called the P50. It is the PO2 at which hemoglobin is 50 percent saturated. **For normal adult blood, under standard conditions,** it is about 27 millimeters of mercury. | P50 guide as v1. **Card title "Normal adult blood", small note "Standard conditions: 37 °C, pH 7.4, PCO2 40 mmHg".** |
| 6 Shape | The top of the curve is **relatively** flat. Even if PO2 in the lungs falls from 100 to 60, saturation only drops to about 90 percent. That plateau protects oxygen loading. Below 60, though, the curve gets steep. In the tissues, a small drop in PO2 releases a large amount of oxygen. That steep slope makes unloading efficient. | Unchanged. |
| 7 Shifts | The curve can also shift. The P50 tells you which way hemoglobin's grip on oxygen has changed. | Unchanged. |
| 8 Right shift | A shift to the right means lower affinity. Hemoglobin lets go of oxygen more easily, and the P50 rises. **Four common factors shift it right.** A lower pH. More carbon dioxide. More 2,3-DPG. And a higher temperature. The effect of acid and carbon dioxide is called the Bohr effect. Think of exercising muscle. It is warm, acidic, and full of carbon dioxide. That is exactly where oxygen needs to be released. | Unchanged (P50 36 marked illustrative). |
| 9 Left shift | A shift to the left is the opposite. Higher affinity. Hemoglobin holds on to oxygen more tightly, and the P50 falls. A higher pH. Less carbon dioxide. Less 2,3-DPG. And a lower temperature. Fetal hemoglobin also sits to the left. It binds 2,3-DPG poorly, which helps the fetus pull oxygen from the mother's blood. **Carbon monoxide is different. It occupies oxygen binding sites, and makes the remaining sites hold oxygen more tightly. So the blood carries less oxygen, and releases less of what it carries.** | Fetal Hb card as v1. **CO card redesigned: mini tetramer with one heme blocked by CO (grey), lines "Blocks O2 binding sites → O2 capacity ↓" and "Remaining sites hold O2 tighter → unloading ↓". Small note: "Curves shown are for normal Hb; with CO the maximum O2 content also falls."** No CO curve is drawn, to avoid the saturation-denominator confusion. |
| 10 Summary | To sum up. Right shift: lower affinity, higher P50, more oxygen released **at a given PO2**. Left shift: higher affinity, lower P50, oxygen held more tightly. An easy way to remember it: right means release. | Final graph and cards. **Right-shift card: "More O2 released at the same PO2".** **Footnote adds: "Total O2 delivery also depends on blood flow, Hb concentration and lung loading."** |
