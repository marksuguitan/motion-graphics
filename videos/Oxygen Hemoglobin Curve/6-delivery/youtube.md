## Description

Hemoglobin has to pick up oxygen in the lungs and let it go in the tissues. One graph shows how it does both: the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve.

In this video for medical students:
• What the axes mean: PO2 (dissolved oxygen) vs hemoglobin saturation
• Why the curve is sigmoid: cooperative binding across hemoglobin's four subunits
• Arterial (PO2 ~100 mmHg, ~97%) and mixed venous (PO2 ~40 mmHg, ~75%) landmarks
• The P50, about 27 mmHg in normal adult blood
• The flat plateau that protects loading and the steep slope that makes unloading efficient
• Right shift (↓pH, ↑CO2, ↑2,3-DPG, ↑temperature; the Bohr effect) and left shift
• Fetal hemoglobin and carbon monoxide

Remember: right means release.
