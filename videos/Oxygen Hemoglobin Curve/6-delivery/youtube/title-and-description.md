## Description

Every red blood cell has two jobs: pick up oxygen in the lungs, then let it go in the tissues. The oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve shows how hemoglobin does both. Cooperative binding gives the curve its S shape. A flat top protects loading in the lungs, and a steep middle makes unloading in the tissues efficient. The P50 tells you which way the curve has shifted. A lower pH, more CO2, more 2,3-DPG and a higher temperature shift it right. Fetal hemoglobin and carbon monoxide sit to the left. Right means release.
